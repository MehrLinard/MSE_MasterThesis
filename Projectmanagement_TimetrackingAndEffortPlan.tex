\subsection*{Two ways of recorded time}
First to some rules as to how time can be registered. Regarding work efforts its important to note that the academic team has an agreement with Swiss Post defining which efforts are considered work time and which not. 
As per agreement, the research part and the writing part of this document are not to be noted as work time, however the remainder of activities such as implementation, documenting work state in Jira or confluence etc. are considered work efforts and are noted into the time tracking at swiss post as such. 



This agreement leads to two different ways of tracking time. At Swiss Post the time is tracked directly with their SAP timetracking \gls{MIAR} system where the efforts are noted towards a specific project account. This makes it easy to export and track them for monitoring purpose in the thesis. 
Second, academic efforts committed to the project outside the agreement were recorded separately. The work was then registed in blocks which are of continuous work on project-related tasks, including short breaks however, longer breaks, such as lunch, resulted in a new block being created. 



\subsection*{Planning review}
At the end of the thesis, the number of hours tracked are summed up per phase they are attributed to by the start and end dates that were effectively reached, takeing into accounts any deviations. These totals per phase and the overall total, lead to a second relative measure. The two relative measures per phase are then compared and evaluated. The summary of this is provided along with the analysis of the planning process at the end of this project management chapter.
